Stripe interview by Chandra

OJ> Hello Stripe ! Please, present yourself...
Stripe> Hi I'm Stripe, I'm 26 years old and have been into computers since the VIC-20. I'm loosing my hair, The staff at the local McDonald knows me too well and I'm into selling PC videoediting systems.

OJ> How many years ago and how did you discover the scene ?
S> Sometime back in 1989 when a friend of mine (Trix/ex. Anarchy) started telling me about trading, copyparties and BBS's.

OJ> You are organizer, sysop (still ?), anything else ?
S> I'm nothing...well around 1991-1992 I was the sysop of a Rebels (later Silents) BBS on the Amiga called High Tension. It ended when I got a mega phonebill. Since then I have just been organizeing The Party.

OJ> In 1991 you decided to create The Party with you brother Ritzy. How did you have this idea ?
S> Ritzy, Trix and I visited the Dexion Xmas party in 1990 and it was cool. But when we came back home I began to think about all the things that the organizers of the Dexion party could have done better. Finally I got so hooked on the idea of making it better, that I started gathering the right people for the job and in the summer of '91 I signed a lease with the local conferencecenter. At that time I was not sure if we could get enough guests to cover the rent, but I just had to try.

OJ> How did you manage to organize this first party ?
S> With a bit of luck and a lot of enthusiasme. At that time it was important to be well known in the scene, so therefor we needed some skilled people from some well known groups. So we ended up composed of people from Crystal (Seen, Paleface), Anarchy (Trix) and Rebels (me). And by spreading the word, the first party became a success. But very hard on the organizers.

OJ> Nowadays The Party is one of the three biggest party in the world, what do you think of this ?
S> Well, it's great that it's possible to get that many talented people together. But the most important about it is still to give everybody a great experience. Whereas it's for some is important to be big, our aim is, and has allways been, to be better. It's doesn't allways work out, but we try our hardest.

OJ> Some people are speaking about "commercial parties". They generally don't consider The Party is one of them. What is a "commercial party" ?
S> A party becomes commercial when the organizers of it no longer does it for the fun, but for the money. When commercial interests decide how and what should happend at a party, then it's all over. And yes, people have also been telling us that we were commercial, but they have never been able to tell us why. Because we don't have the knowledge about getting sponsors, The Party has allways been totally non-commercial, but the sideeffect of that, is that the entrance fee has to cover everything from prizes to renting the conference center. Therefor it has been difficult the keep the entrance fee down.

OJ> What is your best memory ?
S> Ohhh, there's soo many....if I should mention one, it would have to be the prize giving and end ceremony at The Party 1996, I was wery moved when everybody cheered and clapping when it was all over. After that people even came up to me and said thanks for a great party, that was...COOL!

OJ> What is the worse one ?
S> The Party 1991...People were boozing and smashing this and so on...Non of us got any sleep...It really was a nightmare at that time...but the scene said it was too cool, so we hated everything about parties for a couple of months and then started planning the next.

OJ> Do you think there is too much coding parties in the world (near 60th a year) ?
S> No...I belive the parties are an important part of the scene, but I would like to see more parties with better organizing. For me (who's not into coding or anything) there need to be more than just competitions. We can't all be as talented as the elite, but therefor we still like to go to parties.

OJ> How do you feel the future of the scene ?
S> I feel it's moving into the next generation. The old days where the scene was equal to Amiga is over. The scene is expanding and a lot of new people are becoming a part of it. The only drawback is that the majority still have to learn that just because you can code, draw or make music, it doesn't mean that one can make a demo. It takes hard earned skills, coorperation and a story. Showing effects is not enough anymore, a demo has to tell a story to be cool.

OJ> Would you like to work at Microsoft ?
S> he he...who wouldn't like total power ! - well I believe it would be an exiting place to work, but I totally hate any company or organisation that want to, or is controlling the business in the way that Microsoft is. Therefor I only use Microsoft products because I have to. My favorite companies in the computer business is the ones who dares to battle the giants, such as Netscape, Corel and Apple.

OJ> Thank you Stripe, do you want to say a last word ?
S> Hmmmm....Just...be there...we are working on a lot of stuff to make The Party 1997 an extraordinary experience...just hope we'll get it all ready in time...

  
Stripe is the founder of the well-known danish cp "The Party".
