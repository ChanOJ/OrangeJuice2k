OPENGL ? [09 Aug 2000]

Demo Oriented News


------------------


The OpenGL Challenge is proud to present seven impressive "Free Style" entries. These are
real-time visual effects created by programmers from around the world,
to show off what your 3D card can do using OpenGL. First, we have "A
collaboration of NURBS" where dozens of 2 dimensional NURBS writhe and
twist like the tentacles of a jellyfish. A Mac entry named "ExploZ" lets
you fly around a Saturn-type ring expanding from a pulsing sphere. Next
we have another Windows entry "Fly's Eyes" which creates the effect
similar to looking at a sunset through a fly's eyes. "GL Land"
demonstrate water, morphing SuperQuadrics, and a simple sky model using
a unique 3D engine. "Greyscale" shows off a series of fantastic effects
like volumetric layering of a logo, ghost waves, planes of triangles
(this looks cooler than you might imagine), and unique morphing cubes. A
multi-layered "Loading Screen" with a shiny spinning CD, playing music
(Yes, this entry includes music, and is still only 350K!), and pulsing
graphics running in time to the beat. Finally, we have "Smokin' " which
looks much like a ball of swirling smoke and plasma. All these entries
can be downloaded on the Vote page.


Programmer Oriented News

------------------------


The OpenGL Challenge is doing a two-week theme called "Crystalline Structures" where we move
away from soft shapes and enter the hard world of crystalline
structures. If you're interested in writing your first OpenGL program,
this is the perfect place to learn and anonymously show your work to
others. You can find example code from a collection of over 126
challenge entries with all source code released to public domain. Come
join the fun at the OpenGL Challenge, and show off what you can do too.
A few starter ideas for this two week theme; light refraction, spectrums
of colour, solidifying ice crystals, glittering effects of gems,
possibly fractal geometry or your own way of making recursive "edgy"
visual effects. Anything creative is truly welcome, just use your
imagination and a little programming.
