What's that ?
[ >faq / >what is the demoscene ? ]
these are the opinions of chandra (helped by tonic) who thinks (to much) it reflects the general opinion of the scene.

what's a demo ?
a demo is a way to show others what a group can do in programming (that's the coding part), music (zik) or in drawing (gfx), well, it's not exactly drawing, as some use 3ds and pov, so let's say picturing !?
for demos, there are three main kinds or types (encounter of the third type ??).

- Intro 4k : limited size and no music (mostly)
- Intro 64k : limited size, no other restrictions.
- demo : virtualy no limit.

restrictions change from a competition to another, it mostly depends of the machine the programs are runned on (486 or Pentium III ?... :) )

the other types are like
- 128k intro for PC
- 1k small ones for amiga
- TextDemo : text mode ...
- FastIntro : coded during the party with a fixed subject
- Wild Compo : film (VHS...), slide show (images with a bgm (background musik), or weird computers (silicon gaphics (tm) for example).

who are the demomakers ?
they're mostly students; quite young people (between 17 and 30) who are computer addicts spending days (well, nights actually) on coding their demos. the countries known for democoding are Scandinavia (Norway, Sweden and Finland), Germany, The Netherlands and the Frenchies (yep, we too....). some eastern countries (Poland, ...) recently arrived with some pretty good productions. Spain, Italy, Japan and the United States are less active. The other countries are still starting in this domain.
as a demo usually takes quite long to code, (not a day but days, and week, and months...(not years, it would be a game), and as a coder is not always good in composing ziks, work is divided, demomakers group themselves, thus creating demo groups.
a group is like a firm, it has it's own name and reputation, for example: Future Crew, Impact Studios, Pulse (not OJ, we don't code (yet...)).

How to do ?
actually, a group's coder(s) spend(s) his(their) free(or work) time programming small programs (like programming the program with the c programing language and a w95 program in background, that's quite many progs ? isn't it ?). so, these routines (that's how they're named in french (can somebody mail me the english used word ? I've got a blank) (chandra to bbg: the english word is "runtime", little potatoe !)(mairsil to chandra: no no, the english word is "routines" as in french !) are the ones who synchronize the music, display pictures, or do the so called "effects". Routines are usually written in assembler (asm), C/C++ or Pascal. In order to make a good looking demo coders will optimize some parts (the ones who use a lot of cpu time) in asm. C and Pascal, which are "clear" languages are used to link asm routines. Finaly, a few days before the demo's presentation (sometimes (about 3 out of 4) even a few hours or minutes) routines are reunified, and there it is: ..... the demo....

graphics are the work of graphists, and it's a pixel by pixel work... Some even do text art, they use different caracters to make drawings or logos. To complicate the whole, there are two type of text pictures (sorry, encounter of the third type not available this time) there are colored ansi pictures and ascii ones (no colors).

The musician makes, creates and composes the musik that can be heard while pictures and special effect are shown on the screen (35 square meters at a party :) ). it's usually synchronized with the different events of the demo (rythm changes, breaks). zis musik is made with a tracker, a program for writing musik on many sound-tracks, using samples (simply the recording of an instrument or a noise, or whatever). for those who know a bit on computer musik, it's like a midi file, except you don't use the sound-card instrument, but your own. the whole makes a module, it can be recognized by it's extention, mod, s3m, xm and some others which are less common.

present a demo ? where ?
at a coding party ! these assemblies are regularly organized in order to gather the scene (the demomakers) together for several days non-stop (mostly 3 days and 2 nights). organized are: competition for demos, intro, modules (or ziks) and gfx. Some others are the floppy disk throwing competition, same for hard drives or even computers (!) etc.

The biggest parties are the Assembly (Finland), The Party (Danemark) and The Gathering (Norway). They gather 2000 to 4300 people !
But the biggest are not always the best. For example the small (about 400 persons) LTP in France is well considered.

so to get demos, you have to go to coding parties ?
not necessarily. the demos, gfx, and ziks made for (or during) the parties are freely distributed, and can be found on bbs, cd-rom and on the internet (guess where...).
the bbs are modem equipt computers, there, you can get files, or read and participate to conferences dealing with programming and demomakers ;-)
some big bbs' even distribute cd-roms on which the demos
of the year are collected... in France, this is done by ACE.
on internet, there are numerous websites and ftpsites where demos can be downloaded.

and all of it, what computers does it run on ?
everything ! Atari (520, 1040, Falcon), C64, TO7, BeBox, PC (DOS and sometimes Linux, OS/2 and Windows 95), Macintosh, Amiga (500, 600, 1200, ...), Amstrad CPC, Acorn PC, etc...
but the most common (nowadays) are the Amiga and the PC followed by C64, Atari, etc...

now I got it, but what is the scene for ?
since the beginning of personal computers (not only PCs) (early 80') there are pirates (now we call them hackers, but it's not the best word). They modify commercial software (that's cracking) to enable their copiing. And to show their talents, they signed (it's less common these days) their work by adding visual and sound effects at the beginning of the program. after some evolution, the scene was born, the main difference is: the scene has nothing to do with pirating.
